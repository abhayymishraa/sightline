# Sightline icon concepts, v2

Six concepts, drawn from the product brief only. Each concept has a 1024 px SVG and PNG on the macOS grid: an 824 px superellipse body (n = 5), a 100 px margin and a transparent outside. Each concept also has a `-small.svg` variant for 32 px and 16 px. The variant uses thicker strokes, removes fine detail and adds a heavier rim so the body edge stays visible on dark backgrounds. `sheet.html` and `sheet.png` show every concept at 512, 128, 64, 32 and 16 px on #F7F8F8 and #17181C.

Directions: 02 and 04 are minimalist. 03 is industrial telemetry, and 06 is industrial Swiss print. 01 and 05 sit between the two. Five of the six concepts use the brand blue #1E9DF1.

## 01 Plan View
The idea is a top-down view of a desk. A white disc is your head, a blue wedge of attention fans out from it, and the screen it lands on is lit white. The unlit screen is grey. It fits because it shows the full product story (you, the direction you face, the screen that gets focus) without an eye or a monitor glyph. The diagonal wedge also gives it energy without decoration. At 16 px it reads as a dot, a blue beam and a white bar on dark. The lit bar and the dot are the two brightest marks, so the story survives.

## 02 Sightline Caret
The idea is the name drawn literally: a point of view, a tapered ray, and the blue text caret it lands on. It fits the "precise, calm" personality: three marks and one accent on a white body. At 16 px it reads as a black dot and a blue bar, which is enough. The ray softens to a tint. One risk is that the silhouette can suggest a push-pin or a "T".

## 03 Bearing (industrial, telemetry)
The idea is a heading instrument. Three screens sit on the dial, the active one is blue, and the needle is a blue caret that points at it. Square ends and tick marks give it a technical, instrument-panel tone that suits "precise, technical, trustworthy". This concept is the weakest at 16 px. The needle and the lit screen merge into one diagonal stroke, and the icon reads as a generic gauge. At 32 px it is fine. Keep it only if the brand wants the instrument language.

## 04 Lit Pane
The idea is two panes. The pane you face is lit white and holds the caret, and the other pane falls back into the blue. It fits because it maps exactly to "focus moves to the screen, window or split pane you face", and it carries the brand blue at full strength. At 16 px it reads best of the set: a blue tile with one white block. A risk is that, without the caret, it could look like a generic "sidebar layout" glyph. The caret is what makes it Sightline.

## 05 Handoff
The idea is that the caret travels with you. Three afterimages in fading blue lead to the live caret, like a stroboscopic photo of the cursor that moves between screens. It fits "the text cursor follows you" and stays quiet on a dark body. At 32 px the motion reads. At 16 px it becomes a blue gradient block with a bright right edge. That is still recognisable, but the "trail" meaning is mostly lost.

## 06 Slab Track (industrial, Swiss print)
The idea is three screen slabs on a paper substrate, with the active screen in signal red. A selector notch on a heavy track rule marks the active position. This concept argues for an alternative colour. The industrial red reads as "active / armed" and gives the icon a distinct look in a Dock full of blue utility icons. It is also the only concept with no gradients and no radii. At 16 px the slabs and the red block read clearly, and the track becomes a dark line. The small variant fills the inactive slabs solid ink, because 26 px outlines vanish at 16 px.

## Recommendation

1. **01 Plan View.** It is the most ownable and the most "a little clever". It is the only concept that shows the mechanism (head direction becomes focus), and the plan-view drawing avoids every listed cliché. It holds at 16 px as a dot, a beam and a bar.
2. **04 Lit Pane.** It has the strongest small-size and menu-bar legibility of the set, full brand blue, and a calm, literal mapping to "the pane you face gets the caret". It is the safe production choice if 01 feels too illustrative.

A tray or menu-bar glyph can come from either one. From 04, use a one-colour template of the two panes with the right pane filled. From 01, use the dot and the beam.
